Stock Rotation Strategy

A mean reversion-based investment approach applied to individual stocks, systematically rotating capital from stocks near 52-week highs to those near 52-week lows. While transaction costs are higher than ETFs, carefully selected stock combinations can potentially deliver superior returns with increased volatility.

Executive Summary

The Stock Rotation Strategy applies the same mean reversion principle used in ETF rotation but to individual stocks, offering the potential for higher returns in exchange for higher transaction costs and increased volatility. By selecting fundamentally strong stocks across diverse sectors and rotating between them based on 52-week price positioning, this strategy aims to capture significant price normalization opportunities.

Higher Return Potential

Individual stocks can exhibit stronger mean reversion patterns than diversified ETFs, potentially generating higher compound annual growth rates for well-selected stock combinations.

Increased Transaction Costs

Stocks incur higher Securities Transaction Tax (STT) and other charges compared to ETFs. The strategy accounts for these costs in performance calculations to provide realistic expectations.

Greater Volatility

Individual stocks experience higher price fluctuations than index ETFs, leading to larger drawdowns but also bigger potential gains. Risk-adjusted metrics help evaluate if higher returns justify the volatility.

Stock Selection Importance

Success depends significantly on choosing fundamentally sound, liquid stocks with established track records. Diversification across sectors reduces single-stock risk while maintaining rotation benefits.

Key Difference from ETF Strategy: While ETFs provide built-in diversification and lower costs, stocks offer direct exposure to individual company performance. Historical backtests show that despite higher transaction costs (STT of 0.025% on stocks vs 0.001% on ETFs), carefully selected stock combinations can deliver significantly higher returns, albeit with increased volatility and risk.

Strategy Overview

Identical Two-Phase Framework

The Stock Rotation Strategy follows the same operational framework as the ETF strategy:

Phase 1: Accumulation (Weeks 1-52)

Objective: Build a diversified stock portfolio foundation

  • Weekly investment of a fixed capital amount
  • Purchase the stock closest to its 52-week low each week
  • Gradual position building across multiple stocks
  • Lower average entry prices by buying during weakness

Phase 2: Churning (Week 53 onwards)

Objective: Active rotation to capture mean reversion

  • Sell stocks approaching their 52-week highs
  • Redeploy proceeds into stocks near 52-week lows
  • Continuous portfolio optimization through rotation
  • Lock in gains and reinvest in undervalued positions
Example: Suppose you invest ₹50,000 weekly with 8 stocks in your universe. In week 1, if Reliance is closest to its 52-week low, you buy Reliance. In week 2, if ICICI Bank is now closest to its low, you buy ICICI Bank. After 52 weeks, you've built positions across all stocks. From week 53, you actively rotate: selling stocks that have appreciated significantly and are near their highs, and buying those experiencing temporary weakness near their lows.
52-Week Range Analysis & Selection Criteria ▼

Identical Calculation Framework

The stock strategy uses the exact same 252-trading-day window to identify mean reversion opportunities:

52-Week High Maximum closing price in the last 252 trading days
52-Week Low Minimum closing price in the last 252 trading days
Distance from Low (Current Price - 52W Low) / 52W Low × 100
Distance from High (52W High - Current Price) / 52W High × 100

Signal Generation Timeline

  • Signal Date: Friday of each week (or nearest trading day)
  • Execution Date: Monday of the following week (or nearest trading day)
  • Price Used: Monday opening price for all transactions
Selection Logic: During accumulation, the stock with the smallest "distance from 52-week low" is purchased. During churning, stocks are ranked by "distance from 52-week high" and sold in descending order until target capital is raised, then redeployed into the stock closest to its 52-week low.
Transaction Costs & Taxes (Higher for Stocks) ▼

Indian Market Cost Structure for Stocks

Stocks incur higher transaction costs than ETFs, particularly in Securities Transaction Tax:

Cost Component Rate Applied On When
Brokerage User-defined % Transaction Value Buy & Sell
STT (Securities Transaction Tax) 0.025% Transaction Value Sell Only
Stamp Duty 0.015% Transaction Value Buy Only
Exchange Charges 0.00345% Transaction Value Buy & Sell
SEBI Charges 0.0001% Transaction Value Buy & Sell
GST 18% Brokerage Amount Buy & Sell
Cost Comparison: The STT for stocks (0.025%) is 25 times higher than for ETFs (0.001%). Additionally, stamp duty is 3x higher (0.015% vs 0.005%). For a ₹10 lakh sell transaction, ETF STT would be ₹100, while stock STT would be ₹2,500 - a significant difference that impacts net returns.

Capital Gains Tax Implementation

Identical FIFO (First-In-First-Out) methodology as ETF strategy, with 12.5% tax on long-term capital gains:

FIFO Example:
Purchase History for Reliance:
• Batch 1: 50 shares @ ₹2,000 on Jan 1
• Batch 2: 40 shares @ ₹2,200 on Mar 1
• Batch 3: 60 shares @ ₹2,400 on May 1

When selling 70 shares @ ₹2,600:
• First 50 shares use ₹2,000 cost basis: Profit = (2,600-2,000) × 50 = ₹30,000
• Next 20 shares use ₹2,200 cost basis: Profit = (2,600-2,200) × 20 = ₹8,000
• Total Profit = ₹38,000
• Capital Gains Tax = ₹38,000 × 12.5% = ₹4,750

Stock Selection Guidelines ▼

Critical Selection Criteria

Success in stock rotation heavily depends on selecting appropriate stocks:

Market Capitalization Focus on large-cap stocks (₹50,000 Cr+) for adequate liquidity
Liquidity Daily trading volume should support position sizes without slippage
Track Record Minimum 10+ years of trading history for reliable mean reversion patterns
Fundamental Strength Sound business models, consistent profitability, strong balance sheets
Sector Diversification Spread across banking, IT, pharma, FMCG, energy, telecom, etc.
Avoid Speculative Stocks Exclude penny stocks, small-caps, or highly volatile speculative names
Example Universe: A well-diversified stock rotation universe might include blue-chip names like Reliance (Energy), ICICI Bank (Banking), TCS (IT), Hindustan Unilever (FMCG), Bharti Airtel (Telecom), Cipla (Pharma), Bajaj Finance (NBFC), and Britannia (Consumer). These represent established leaders with long track records and sufficient liquidity.

Stocks to Avoid

  • Small/Mid-caps: Higher risk of non-recovery from lows
  • Low Liquidity: Execution slippage can erode strategy returns
  • Cyclical Extremes: Stocks in structurally declining industries
  • High Debt: Companies with weak financial health
  • Regulatory Issues: Stocks facing legal or compliance problems
Advantages, Risks & Considerations ▼

Key Advantages

  • Higher Return Potential: Well-selected stock combinations can significantly outperform broad market indices
  • Stronger Mean Reversion: Individual stocks often exhibit more pronounced price normalization than diversified ETFs
  • Sector-Specific Opportunities: Ability to capture sector rotation dynamics
  • Direct Company Exposure: Benefit from individual company performance and growth
  • Systematic Discipline: Rules-based approach prevents emotional stock picking

Important Risks

  • Higher Volatility: Expect larger portfolio value fluctuations and deeper drawdowns
  • Increased Costs: Higher STT and other charges reduce net returns compared to ETFs
  • Company-Specific Risk: Individual stocks can face business disruption, management issues, or sector headwinds
  • Liquidity Risk: Some stocks may have insufficient liquidity during market stress
  • Selection Risk: Poor stock selection can lead to permanently impaired capital
  • Concentration Risk: Smaller universe means less diversification than broad index ETFs

When to Prefer Stock Rotation

Consider stock rotation if you:
• Have higher risk tolerance and can withstand 30-40%+ drawdowns
• Seek higher returns and are willing to accept increased volatility
• Have the discipline to stick with the strategy during difficult periods
• Understand individual company fundamentals and sector dynamics
• Have sufficient capital to maintain diversification across 6-10 stocks
• Can monitor portfolio regularly for any structural changes in companies

When to Prefer ETF Rotation

Stick with ETF rotation if you:
• Prefer lower volatility and smaller drawdowns
• Want built-in diversification with lower costs
• Have limited time for stock-specific research
• Seek steadier, more predictable returns
• Are relatively new to systematic rotation strategies
