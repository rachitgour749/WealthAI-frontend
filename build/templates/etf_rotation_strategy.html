ETF Rotation Strategy

A mean reversion-based investment approach that systematically rotates capital between Exchange Traded Funds (ETFs), buying undervalued positions near 52-week lows and selling when they recover toward 52-week highs, capturing price normalization while managing risk through diversification.

Executive Summary

The ETF Rotation Strategy is built on the fundamental principle of mean reversion - the statistical tendency for asset prices to return to their average value over time. The strategy systematically identifies ETFs trading near their 52-week lows (representing potential undervaluation) and rotates capital away from ETFs near 52-week highs (representing potential overvaluation), capturing price normalization cycles.

Low Transaction Costs

ETFs offer significantly lower transaction costs compared to individual stocks, making frequent rotation economically viable even after accounting for all charges and taxes.

Systematic Approach

The strategy eliminates emotional decision-making by following a rules-based framework that identifies buy and sell opportunities based on 52-week price momentum.

Risk Management

Weekly rebalancing ensures portfolio diversification across multiple ETFs, reducing concentration risk while capturing opportunities across different market sectors.

Tax Efficiency

The strategy implements FIFO (First-In-First-Out) accounting with comprehensive capital gains tax tracking, ensuring full compliance with Indian tax regulations.

Why Mean Reversion Works: Financial markets exhibit cyclical behavior where prices oscillate around long-term averages. ETFs near their 52-week lows often represent temporary undervaluation due to market overreaction, sector rotation, or short-term negative sentiment. Similarly, ETFs near 52-week highs may be temporarily overextended. By systematically buying depressed assets and selling appreciated ones, the strategy captures the natural tendency of prices to revert to their mean, while ETF's low-cost structure ensures transaction expenses don't erode these gains.

Strategy Overview

Two-Phase Operation

Phase 1: Accumulation (Weeks 1-52)

Objective: Build a diversified ETF portfolio foundation

  • Weekly investment of a fixed capital amount
  • Purchase the ETF closest to its 52-week low each week
  • Gradual position building across multiple ETFs
  • Lower average entry prices by buying during weakness

Phase 2: Churning (Week 53 onwards)

Objective: Active rotation to capture momentum shifts

  • Sell ETFs approaching their 52-week highs
  • Redeploy proceeds into ETFs near 52-week lows
  • Continuous portfolio optimization
  • Lock in gains and reinvest in value opportunities
Example: Suppose you invest ₹10,000 weekly. In week 1, if ETF_A is closest to its 52-week low at ₹100 (compared to its high of ₹150), you purchase ETF_A. In week 2, if ETF_B is now closest to its low, you buy ETF_B. After 52 weeks, you've built a diversified portfolio. From week 53, you actively rotate: selling ETFs that have appreciated and are near their highs, and buying those that are near their lows.
52-Week Range Analysis & Selection Criteria ▼

52-Week Range Framework

The strategy uses a 252-trading-day window (approximately one year) to identify mean reversion opportunities by measuring price positions relative to their annual range:

52-Week High Maximum closing price in the last 252 trading days
52-Week Low Minimum closing price in the last 252 trading days
Distance from Low (Current Price - 52W Low) / 52W Low × 100
Distance from High (52W High - Current Price) / 52W High × 100

Signal Generation Timeline

  • Signal Date: Friday of each week (or nearest trading day if Friday is a holiday)
  • Execution Date: Monday of the following week (or nearest trading day if Monday is a holiday)
  • Price Used: Monday opening price for all transactions
Selection Logic: In the accumulation phase, the ETF with the smallest "distance from 52-week low" is selected for purchase. In the churning phase, ETFs are ranked by "distance from 52-week high" and sold in that order until the target capital is raised.

Capital Gains Tax Implementation

The strategy uses FIFO (First-In-First-Out) methodology to track cost basis and calculate capital gains tax at 12.5% on profits:

FIFO Example:
Purchase History for ETF_A:
• Batch 1: 100 units @ ₹50 on Jan 1
• Batch 2: 150 units @ ₹55 on Feb 1
• Batch 3: 200 units @ ₹60 on Mar 1

When selling 180 units @ ₹65:
• First 100 units use ₹50 cost basis: Profit = (65-50) × 100 = ₹1,500
• Next 80 units use ₹55 cost basis: Profit = (65-55) × 80 = ₹800
• Total Profit = ₹2,300
• Capital Gains Tax = ₹2,300 × 12.5% = ₹287.50

Net Cash Flow: For purchases, net amount = transaction value + costs. For sales, net amount = transaction value - costs - capital gains tax. This ensures your portfolio reflects actual available capital after all expenses.

Strategy Advantages & Considerations ▼

Key Advantages

  • Systematic Discipline: Removes emotional bias from investment decisions through rules-based execution
  • Cost Efficiency: ETFs have lower expense ratios and transaction costs compared to actively managed funds
  • Diversification: Automatically builds exposure across multiple ETFs and sectors
  • Mean Reversion Capture: Exploits the statistical tendency of prices to return to average levels
  • Transparent Accounting: All costs, taxes, and performance metrics clearly tracked
  • Weekly Rebalancing: Regular opportunities to optimize portfolio composition

Important Considerations

  • Mean Reversion Validity: Strategy assumes prices tend to revert to their average over the measurement period
  • Transaction Frequency: Higher turnover than buy-and-hold generates more costs and tax events
  • Market Conditions: Performance may vary in trending vs. range-bound markets
  • Liquidity Requirements: Assumes sufficient ETF trading volumes for execution without market impact
  • Data Requirements: Requires minimum 252 days of historical data for accurate range calculation

Backtesting Limitations

Important Note: Historical backtesting assumes perfect execution at Monday opening prices without slippage. Real-world results may vary due to market impact, liquidity constraints, and execution timing. The strategy only includes ETFs with complete data history, which may introduce survivorship bias.
