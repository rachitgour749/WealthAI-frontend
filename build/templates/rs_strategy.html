Relative Strength (RS) Strategy

A momentum-driven investment approach that identifies and holds the strongest performing stocks from the Nifty 500 universe by measuring their relative strength against the Nifty 50 index. This strategy systematically rides market winners while they maintain their outperformance momentum.

Executive Summary

The Relative Strength (RS) Strategy is a momentum-based approach that capitalizes on a powerful market phenomenon: stocks that are outperforming the market tend to continue outperforming in the near term. By systematically identifying and holding the top performers from the Nifty 500 universe relative to Nifty 50, this strategy aims to capture sustained upward trends.

Momentum Capture

Identifies stocks demonstrating the strongest relative performance across multiple timeframes (1 week, 1 month, 1 quarter) to capture sustained momentum trends.

Bull Market Advantage

This strategy performs exceptionally well during bull phases when market momentum is strong and stock prices exhibit trending behavior with clear winners emerging.

Systematic Selection

Removes subjective bias by using a rules-based ranking system to identify top performers, with weekly rebalancing to ensure portfolio always holds current market leaders.

Risk Management

Built-in stop-loss mechanisms and automatic exits when stocks lose their relative strength ranking protect capital during momentum reversals.

Why Momentum Works: Financial markets exhibit "persistence of performance" - stocks that have been rising strongly tend to continue rising in the near term due to factors like positive investor sentiment, improving fundamentals, institutional accumulation, and sector tailwinds. This strategy systematically identifies these winners before the momentum exhausts, positioning your portfolio to benefit from this well-documented market behavior. This approach is particularly powerful during bull market phases when clear trends emerge and momentum persists for extended periods.
Single vs Dual Momentum: This strategy implements single momentum by measuring stock performance only against the Nifty 50 benchmark. A more advanced variant would implement dual momentum by additionally measuring stocks against their sector indices (e.g., Bank Nifty for banking stocks, IT Index for IT stocks), capturing both broad market momentum and sector-specific momentum. The dual momentum approach can further enhance returns by identifying stocks that are leaders both in their sector and the broader market.

Strategy Overview

Core Concept: Relative Strength

Relative Strength (RS) measures how well a stock is performing compared to a benchmark index (Nifty 50 in this case). Unlike absolute returns which just show if a stock went up or down, RS shows if a stock is outperforming or underperforming the market.

Relative Strength Formula:
RS = (Stock Return / Index Return) - 1
Interpretation:
RS > 0 = Stock is outperforming the index (Momentum!)
RS = 0 = Stock is matching the index
RS < 0 = Stock is underperforming the index
Example: Suppose over the last month, Reliance gained 8% while Nifty 50 gained 5%. The relative strength would be (1.08/1.05) - 1 = 0.0286 or +2.86%. This positive RS indicates Reliance is outperforming the market, making it a candidate for inclusion in the portfolio. Conversely, if HDFC Bank gained only 2% in the same period, its RS would be (1.02/1.05) - 1 = -0.0286 or -2.86%, indicating underperformance.

Multi-Timeframe RS Score

Rather than relying on a single timeframe, the strategy calculates RS across three periods to get a comprehensive view of momentum:

Short-term (1 Week) Captures immediate momentum and recent price action
Medium-term (1 Month) Identifies sustained trends over the past month
Long-term (1 Quarter) Confirms longer-term outperformance patterns
Composite RS Score Average of all three timeframes for robust ranking

The RS Score is the average of these three RS measurements, providing a balanced view that captures both recent momentum and sustained trends. Stocks are ranked by this composite score to identify the strongest performers.

How the Strategy Works

Weekly Cycle

The strategy operates on a disciplined weekly schedule to systematically capture momentum:

1
Friday (Signal Generation) At the end of Friday's trading session, the strategy analyzes all Nifty 500 stocks, calculating their RS Score against Nifty 50 across the three timeframes. Stocks are ranked from highest to lowest RS Score.
2
Weekend (Order Preparation) The system determines which stocks to buy (top ranked with positive RS) and which to sell (existing holdings that have fallen out of top ranks or hit stop loss).
3
Monday (Execution) At Monday's market open, exit orders are executed first (selling underperformers), followed by entry orders (buying new top performers). All trades use market orders at opening prices.
4
Week Ahead (Hold Period) Portfolio holds the selected stocks through the week. Daily monitoring for stop-loss violations, but no intra-week rebalancing. Process repeats every Friday.

Stock Universe

The strategy selects from the Nifty 500 universe - India's 500 largest and most liquid companies. This provides:

  • Broad Coverage: Access to opportunities across all sectors and market capitalizations
  • Quality Filter: All stocks are established companies with proven track records
  • Liquidity: Easy to enter and exit positions without significant market impact
  • Sufficient Choices: Large enough universe to always find strong momentum candidates
Entry & Exit Rules ▼

When to Buy (Entry Conditions)

Entry Criteria

  • Positive RS Score: Only consider stocks with RS Score > 0 (outperforming Nifty 50)
  • Top Ranked: Select from the highest-ranked stocks by RS Score
  • Portfolio Capacity: Buy only if there are vacant positions (e.g., if max holdings is 10 and you hold 8, buy 2 more)
  • Equal Weighting: Allocate equal capital to each position based on configured position size
Example: Suppose your strategy is configured to hold maximum 10 stocks with 10% position size each. On Friday, if you're holding only 7 stocks and 3 positions are vacant, the strategy will identify the top 10 ranked stocks with positive RS scores. The 3 new stocks (not already held) with the highest rankings will be purchased on Monday morning, each receiving 10% allocation.

When to Sell (Exit Conditions)

RS Rank Exit If a held stock's rank falls below your maximum holdings threshold (e.g., if holding 10 stocks max and it falls to rank 11 or lower), sell on next Monday.
Stop Loss Exit If stock price falls by the configured stop-loss percentage from your entry price, exit immediately at next day's open.
Negative RS Exit If a held stock's RS Score turns negative (underperforming the market), it automatically exits the ranking and triggers a sell.
Max Holding Period Optional exit after configured maximum holding period (e.g., 12 weeks) even if still ranked well.

Tie-Breaking Rules

When multiple stocks have identical RS Scores, the strategy uses these criteria in order:

  1. Higher short-term RS (1-week): Prefer stocks with stronger recent momentum
  2. Higher medium-term RS (1-month): If still tied, prefer sustained monthly momentum
  3. Lower volatility: Choose more stable performer over choppy one
  4. Higher market cap: Prefer larger, more liquid stocks

When cumulative profit or loss exceeds a threshold (e.g., ±10% of total capital), the strategy resets:

  • Recalculated Allocations: Position sizes adjusted based on new capital
  • Prevents Concentration: Ensures positions stay proportional to current capital

Risk Management & Stop Loss ▼

Stop Loss Protection

The strategy implements a configurable stop-loss mechanism to protect capital:

How Stop Loss Works

  • Trigger Level: Set at a percentage below entry price (e.g., 8-10%)
  • Monitoring: Checked daily based on closing prices
  • Execution: If closing price breaches stop loss, exit at next day's opening price
  • Gap Handling: If market gaps down through stop loss, exit at open price and record slippage
Example: You buy TCS at ₹3,500 with a 10% stop loss. Your stop loss level is ₹3,150. If TCS closes at ₹3,140 on any day, a sell order is triggered for the next morning. If the market opens at ₹3,100 (gap down), the exit occurs at ₹3,100, and the ₹40 additional loss is recorded as slippage.

Why Stop Loss is Critical

  • Momentum Reversal: Protects when stock momentum suddenly reverses
  • Capital Preservation: Limits loss on any single position
  • Portfolio Protection: Prevents one bad stock from severely impacting overall returns
  • Psychological Benefit: Removes emotional decision-making during drawdowns

Portfolio-Level Risk Controls

Diversification Multiple positions (5-10 stocks) prevent concentration risk
Weekly Rebalance Regular review ensures holdings still meet criteria
Positive RS Filter Only holds stocks currently outperforming market
Automatic Exits Systematic removal of underperformers

When This Strategy Excels

Bull Market Performance: The RS Strategy is specifically designed to capitalize on bull market conditions. When the market is in an uptrend with clear momentum, stocks tend to exhibit sustained directional moves, making relative strength patterns highly profitable. During such phases, winners keep winning and the strategy systematically captures these trends. Historical data shows momentum strategies can deliver exceptional returns during prolonged bull phases, often significantly outperforming buy-and-hold approaches.

Optimal Market Conditions

  • Strong Uptrends: Clear market direction with rising indices
  • High Breadth: Many stocks participating in the rally
  • Sector Rotation: Leadership shifting between sectors provides fresh opportunities
  • Low Volatility: Steady trends without excessive whipsaws

Challenging Market Conditions

  • Range-Bound Markets: Sideways movement without clear trends reduces momentum effectiveness
  • High Volatility: Frequent reversals can trigger stop losses and reduce returns
  • Bear Markets: Downtrends cause most stocks to have negative RS scores, limiting opportunities

Single vs Dual Momentum Enhancement ▼

Current Implementation: Single Momentum

This strategy currently implements single momentum by measuring stock performance exclusively against the Nifty 50 index. This provides a straightforward and effective way to identify market leaders.

Single Momentum Example: HDFC Bank is compared only to Nifty 50. If HDFC Bank gained 12% while Nifty 50 gained 8%, it shows positive relative strength and ranks well. The sector it belongs to (banking) is not separately considered in the current implementation.

Advanced Variation: Dual Momentum

An enhanced version of this strategy would implement dual momentum by measuring stocks against both the broad market index AND their respective sector indices:

Broad Market RS Stock performance vs Nifty 50 (existing calculation)
Sector RS Stock performance vs its sector index (new addition)
Combined Score Weighted average of both RS measurements
Dual Filter Stock must outperform both benchmarks
Dual Momentum Example: Consider HDFC Bank during a period when:
• HDFC Bank gained 12%
• Nifty 50 gained 8% → Market RS = +4% (positive)
• Bank Nifty gained 15% → Sector RS = -3% (negative)

In single momentum, HDFC Bank would rank well (+4% market RS).
In dual momentum, HDFC Bank might be excluded or ranked lower because it's underperforming its banking peers, even while beating the broader market. This additional filter helps identify not just market leaders, but sector leaders.

Benefits of Dual Momentum

  • Sector Leadership: Captures stocks that are leaders within their industry
  • Enhanced Returns: Studies show dual momentum can improve performance by 2-3% CAGR
  • Better Risk-Adjusted Returns: Reduces false positives from sector-wide movements
  • Sector Rotation Capture: Automatically shifts to sectors showing strong momentum

Considerations for Dual Momentum

  • More Complex: Requires sector classification and multiple benchmarks
  • Fewer Opportunities: Stricter filters mean fewer qualifying stocks
  • Higher Turnover: More frequent rebalancing as sector leadership shifts
  • Implementation: Currently planned as a future enhancement to MarketsAI1
